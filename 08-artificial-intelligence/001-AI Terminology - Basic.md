## 1. Artificial Intelligence (AI)

AI is computer technology that allows machines to think, learn, and solve problems like humans.

Think of it as teaching a computer to **think, learn, or make decisions**.

**Example:**

- ChatGPT answering questions
- Google Maps finding the best route
- Netflix recommending movies

## 2. Machine Learning (ML)

Machine Learning is a way of teaching computers using data instead of writing every rule manually.

The more data it sees, the better it becomes.

**Example:**

You don’t tell Gmail every spam email.

Instead, Gmail learns by looking at millions of emails.

## 3. Deep Learning (DL)

Deep Learning is an advanced type of Machine Learning that uses large networks to solve difficult problems.

It is especially good at:

- Images
- Voice
- Language
- Video

**Example:**

Face Unlock on your phone uses Deep Learning.

## 4. Neural Network

A Neural Network is the “brain” that helps AI learn patterns.

It is inspired by the human brain but is much simpler.

**Example:**

When AI recognizes a cat in a photo, a Neural Network is doing the work.

## 5. Large Language Model (LLM)

An LLM is an AI model trained on a huge amount of text.

It learns how humans write and speak.

**Examples**

- ChatGPT
- Gemini
- Claude
- Llama

## 6. Small Language Model (SLM)

An SLM works like an LLM but is much smaller.

It runs faster and can often work directly on your phone or laptop.

**Example**

Google AI Edge models running on Android devices.

## 7. Foundation Model

A Foundation Model is a general-purpose AI model.

It learns many different tasks first and can later be customized for specific jobs.

Think of it as a student who learns every subject before choosing a career.

## 8. Generative AI

Generative AI creates new content instead of simply finding existing information.

It can generate:

- Text
- Images
- Music
- Videos
- Code

**Example**

Ask ChatGPT to write an email or create Python code.

## 9. Multimodal AI

Multimodal AI understands more than one type of information.

It can work with:

- Text
- Images
- Audio
- Video

**Example**

Upload a photo to ChatGPT and ask, “What’s happening here?”

## 10. Edge AI

Edge AI runs directly on a device instead of sending data to the cloud.

This makes it:

- Faster
- More private
- Works offline

**Example**

A security camera detecting people without using the internet.

## 11. On-Device AI

AI that runs completely on your phone, laptop, or tablet.

No internet is required.

**Example**

Live translation on a smartphone.

## 12. Model

A model is the AI itself.

It has already learned from data and is ready to answer questions or perform tasks.

Think of it as a trained employee ready to work.

## 13. Training

Training is the process of teaching an AI model.

The model studies millions or even billions of examples.

Just like students study before exams.

## 14. Inference

Inference is when the trained AI actually starts working.

Training is learning.

Inference is using what it has learned.

**Example**

When ChatGPT answers your question, it is performing inference.

## 15. Fine-Tuning

Fine-tuning means teaching an already trained AI a new specialty.

Instead of starting from scratch, you improve an existing model.

**Example**

Teaching an AI to become a medical assistant after it has already learned general knowledge.

## 16. Quantization

Quantization makes AI models smaller and faster.

The goal is to reduce memory usage while keeping good accuracy.

**Example**

[](https://medium.com/write?source=promotion_paragraph---post_body_banner_home_for_stories_scribble--f92512ac99c8---------------------------------------)

A model that needs 8 GB of memory may only need 2 GB after quantization.

## 17. Distillation

Distillation is like a teacher helping a student.

A large AI teaches a smaller AI to perform almost as well.

The smaller model becomes faster and cheaper.

## 18. Token

AI doesn’t read whole sentences.

It breaks everything into small pieces called **tokens**.

A token might be:

- A word
- Part of a word
- A punctuation mark

Think of LEGO bricks.

Many small pieces build a complete sentence.

## 19. Tokenization

Tokenization is the process of splitting text into tokens.

Before AI understands your message, it first breaks it into small pieces.

## 20. Context Window

This is the amount of information AI can remember during one conversation.

Think of it as a whiteboard.

Once the whiteboard is full, older information starts disappearing.

## 21. Prompt

A prompt is simply your instruction to the AI.

It tells the AI what you want.

**Example**

“Write a professional email.”

That’s a prompt.

## 22. Prompt Engineering

Prompt Engineering means writing better prompts to get better answers.

Clear questions usually produce clear results.

## 23. System Prompt

A hidden instruction that tells the AI how it should behave.

**Example**

“You are an experienced software architect.”

This changes how the AI answers.

## 24. Temperature

Temperature controls how creative the AI becomes.

- Low temperature → More factual
- High temperature → More creative

Think of it as a creativity slider.

## 25. Top-K

Top-K limits the AI to choosing from only the top few possible next words.

This keeps responses more focused.

## 26. Top-P

Top-P lets the AI choose from the most likely words until a certain probability is reached.

It creates a balance between creativity and accuracy.

## 27. Hallucination

Sometimes AI confidently gives wrong information.

This is called a hallucination.

It isn’t lying — it simply predicts text that sounds correct but isn’t.

==Always verify important information.==

## 28. Memory

Memory helps AI remember useful information during or across conversations.
Without memory, every conversation starts fresh.

## 29. Embedding

An embedding is a numerical representation of meaning.

Instead of storing words, AI stores their meaning as numbers.

Words with similar meanings end up close together.

## 30. Vector

A vector is simply a list of numbers.

Those numbers help AI compare meanings.

Think of it as a unique fingerprint for information.

## 31. Vector Database

A Vector Database stores vectors instead of normal text.

This allows AI to quickly find information with similar meaning.

Examples include Pinecone, Weaviate, and Milvus.

## 32. Semantic Search

Semantic Search looks for meaning instead of exact words.

**Example**

Searching for:

_“cheap phone”_

might also find

_“affordable smartphone.”_

Even though the words are different, the meaning is similar.

## 33. Chunking

Chunking means splitting large documents into smaller pieces.

AI reads one small section at a time instead of an entire book.

Think of cutting a large pizza into slices before eating it.

## 34. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

RAG allows AI to search for information before answering.

Instead of relying only on what it learned during training, it first looks up relevant documents.

Think of it like an open-book exam: the AI checks trusted notes before responding.

**Example**

A company chatbot searches its internal manuals before answering an employee’s question.

## 35. Context Engineering

Context Engineering is the practice of giving AI the **right information** before asking it to solve a problem.

Instead of only writing a better prompt, you provide the AI with everything it needs, such as:

- Background information
- Documents
- Examples
- Rules
- Previous conversation

The better the context, the better the answer.

Think of it this way:

A chef can cook a great meal only if you provide the right ingredients. AI works the same way.
